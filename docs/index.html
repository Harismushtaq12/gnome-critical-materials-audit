Does AI materials discovery lean on critical raw materials?

Google DeepMind's GNoME proposed about 554,000 new stable materials. This page compares the elements they are made of with materials that are already known, using public data only.

3 · Why it happens

Mostly a choice of elements, not only a choice of compound type

GNoME explores many large metal-only compounds. That explains part of the gap. The rest comes from which elements it picks within the same type of compound.

How much of the gap comes from compound type

Light part: explained by GNoME's mix of compound types. Dark part: remaining gap within the same type. Common support, compared with known materials.

Use compared with random element choice

1.0 means the family appears as often as if elements were picked at random. Known materials use these families less than random; GNoME uses them more.

6 · Limits

What this study cannot show

  1. It looks at which elements materials contain, not whether they can be made. Checking that needs calculations that treat 4f electrons explicitly, or experiments.
  2. The fair comparison covers 72.6% of GNoME. Very large metal-only compounds have almost no known counterparts.
  3. DeepMind's r²SCAN re-check covers 117,043 materials that are not a random sample (fewer rare earths than the rest).
  4. USGS reports rare earths as one group, and Ru, Rh, Os and Ir together with platinum; germanium has no world figure.
  5. Hypotheses H1–H3 were fixed before the real data. Everything else is exploratory. The alternatives analysis was redesigned once after its first run.
  6. On fake data with no effect, the tests gave false alarms of the time, close to the expected 5%.
